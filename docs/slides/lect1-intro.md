## Slide 2: What is AI?

According to John McCarthy, one of the founders of AI:

> AI is the science and engineering of making intelligent machines, especially intelligent computer programs. It is related to the similar task of using computers to understand human intelligence, but AI does not have to confine itself to methods that are biologically observable.

## Slide 4: Three Aspects of AI

### 1. Motivation and long-term goals

- AI researchers want to make machines, especially computer programs, that can do things for humans which so far can only be done by humans.
- In computer science, AI is at the forefront of computer applications.
- AI's long-term goal is to build machines that can sense, think, and act intelligently.

### 2. Techniques and algorithms from AI

Examples include:

- Heuristic search algorithms
- Knowledge representation languages and reasoners
- Machine learning algorithms

### 3. Impacts of AI

AI affects:

- Other sciences and engineering fields, including mathematics, physics, chemistry, and biology
- Everyday life
- Humanity in general

## Slide 7: How It Got Started - Ada Lovelace

Ada Lovelace (1815-1852) was the only child of the poet Lord Byron.

Her Note G describes an algorithm for Babbage's Analytical Engine to compute Bernoulli numbers, and is considered the first published computer program.

She wrote that the Analytical Engine has no power to originate anything. It can do whatever humans know how to order it to perform. It can follow analysis, but it has no power of anticipating analytical relations or truths.

## Slide 8: How It Got Started - Alan Turing

Alan Turing (1912-1954) is often called the father of computer science.

Reference:

- Alan Turing. "Computing Machinery and Intelligence." *Mind*, 59:433-460, 1950.

Turing begins by considering the question: "Can machines think?"

## Slide 9: How It Got Started - Dartmouth Conference

The Dartmouth Conference in 1956 was proposed by John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester, and Claude Shannon.

The proposal described a two-month study of artificial intelligence at Dartmouth College. It was based on the conjecture that every aspect of learning or any feature of intelligence can in principle be precisely described, so that a machine can be made to simulate it.

This was the first use of the term "artificial intelligence", coined by McCarthy.

## Slide 11: New Problem-Solving Methodology Using Generative AI

AI is the science and engineering of making intelligent computer programs and is at the frontier of computer applications.

AI gives new ways to solve problems on computers, including new methodologies and algorithms.

Example programming task:

1. The total number of words or Chinese characters in the file.
2. The line numbers of the shortest and longest lines.

## Slide 12: Course Positioning

The lecture positions AI as both:

- A long-term scientific and engineering project to build intelligent machines.
- A practical source of methods for solving computational problems.

Key takeaway:

Students should learn both the current applications of AI and the underlying AI methods that may remain useful beyond any single generation of popular tools.
